Overview

Rangeley is a city in Maine with a population of 1,200 in 2012. Rangeley is the center of the Rangeley Lakes Region, a resort area in Franklin County. It includes the villages of Oquossoc, Haines Landing and South Rangeley

Get in

By car

The ride into Rangeley is not too bad, but coming from the south (lower New England), can be a little frustrating. From the Maine Turnpike, it is necessary to take the Rt. 4 exit. Up until this point, the ride is fine. The ride up Rt. 4 is roughly two hours into Rangeley and has some hilly, windy roads.

By public transit

The closest real transit connection is Lewiston–Auburn, which connects to the Portland Amtrak station via the LAP bus with multiple services per day. From Lewiston it's easiest to drive (or have someone drive you) the 2 hours into town.

The truly committed can then use the once-monthly Western Maine Transportation bus to connect Lewiston to Farmington and eventually to Rangeley.

By foot

The nearby Appalachian Trail brings people walking from Georgia. It's a bit of a slog into town along a busy Rt 4 with no sidewalk. Drivers are friendly and will often pick up hikers who want to hitchhike into town.

Get around

By car

Most people in the region get around by driving. It's the most practical way to get to the many cottages, hikes, and surrounding small towns.

Parking downtown can be tricky during a busy summer afternoon, but is generally easy.

By foot

The town itself is compact and easy to walk between stores once you're here. Outside of downtown, the roads have little berm, no sidewalks, high speed limits, and large logging trucks.

By bike

In summer, committed cyclists bike around the lake. Shops in town also rent fat tire bikes to go on the snowmobile trails during the summertime.

By snowmobile, ATV

Maine Snowmobile Association ITS trails go throughout the region. They are a scenic way to get around during the winter (with a snowmobile) and summer (with an ATV).

By public transit

The monthly, reservation-required Western Maine Transportation bus connects Rangeley to surrounding towns.

Otherwise, none exists; it's a small town.

See

The mountain ranges that can be seen from the heart of Rangeley are simply breath-taking. Among the mountains, Saddleback is the most well known, and it is the mountain that people ski on. Another well-known mountain that can be seen is Sugerloaf Mountain. Rangeley Lake is a very large lake, spanning roughly ten miles long in the longest section, is a great site as well.

Do Snowmobiling

There are hundreds of miles of available to snowmobile on, with great attractions along the way. There are many restaurants accessible right from the trails, and there are many great "picture perfect" sights along the way. In the winter, the lake freezes up and can be snowmobiled on as well. Do Rangeley Lake

In the summer Rangeley Lake is a great place to be. The lake is very large, varying in depth, but it is said to go as deep as 200-250 feet deep in some of the underwater canyons. It is fun to boat on the lake, water-ski, jet-ski, and go tubing. There are even some seaplane tours available to the public.

Sleep

Many owners of lakefront cabins in the Rangeley area use them as vacation homes and choose to rent them to visitors when not in use. These cabins can be an exceptional value and offer many of the amenities of home and convenient access to water recreation.

Stay Rangeley Inn

Combination of a large, historic country inn and a more contemporary motel situated on Haley Pond in downtown Rangeley. The historic inn rooms in the main building have great ambiance, but are frequently not available in the winter off-season; the motel rooms have been renovated and are a good year-round alternative. Stay safe

The only time that you should worry about getting out of Rangeley is during snowstorms. Traveling down Rt. 4 in the snow can be very frightening. However traffic is never really a huge concern. Be careful of the huge logging trucks as they travel very fast!
