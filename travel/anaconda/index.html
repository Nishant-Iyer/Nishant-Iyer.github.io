Overview

Anaconda is a city of about 9,400 people (2020) in southwestern Montana. It is in a valley of the Deer Lodge National Forest, giving impressive views and plenty of nearby outdoor activities.

Best things about Anaconda

Washoe Theater

Opened in 1936, the last theater constructed in the United States in the Art Deco style. It shows films and periodically hosts plays and other entertainment.

Anaconda Stack State Park

The Anaconda Smelter Stack is 585 feet (178 m) tall, one of the tallest free standing masonry structures in the world. It cannot be visited but can be seen and photographed from the information plaque at this address.

Copper Village Museum and Arts Center and Marcus Daly Historical Museum

Exhibitions of contemporary Montana artists and a variety of tradition and heritage arts. Also houses a collection about Marcus Daly. Hosts the annual Art in Washoe Park, a free 3-day event every July.

Benny Goodman Park

Community park with a skatepark professionally designed by Dreamland. Pit open to skateboards, inline skates, bikes, and scooters.

    Understand

    Anaconda was founded by Marcus Daly, one of the Copper Kings. He constructed a smelter on nearby Warm Springs Creek to process copper ore from the Butte mines. The work drew immigrants from a variety of countries. This made Anaconda one of the more diverse towns in early Montana.

    Get in

    State Route 1 (MT-1) runs east-west through the city.

    Interstate 90 passes about 5 mi east of the city, near Butte; take Exit 208 to merge onto MT-1.

    Get around

    The main road splits into 2 one-way streets in the downtown area, Park Ave and Commercial Ave.

    There is no public transportation in Anaconda. The main part of town is easy to walk or bike.
