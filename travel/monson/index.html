Overview

Monson is a small town in Maine, with a population of 609 according to the 2020 census. It was incorporated in 1822, and named after Monson, Massachusetts. Monson is primarily known for its slate quarries and it being the last town before the 100 mile wilderness on the Appalachian Trail. Today it has become something of an artist's town featuring galleries and several restaurants while still catering to thru hikers of the AT.

    Get in

    Hike in on the Appalachian Trail.

    The primary road into Monson is Route 6, sometimes referred to as Route 15. This road goes directly through town and eventually leads to Greenville.

    Get around

    There is no public transportation in Monson like much of northern Maine. Once you're in town you'll need to walk, drive or ride to traverse the area. Most if not all of main street has sidewalks and is wheelchair accessible.

    Do

    Hike on the AT, which used to go through town, but now bypasses on a ridge 2 miles west.

    See Borestone Mountain Audubon Sanctuary (Borestone Mountain)

    Borestone has four trails each offering amazing views and open dependent on time of year and weather conditions. These trails include the 1.1 mile Base trail which crosses an access road several times on the way up to the visitor center, the Summit trail (1 Mile to west peak, and 1.3 miles to east peak), Fox Pen Loop Approx. 0.5 miles, and the Peregrine Trail (0.5 miles). Always check with the visitor center to make sure the trail is open.

    Sleep

    Stay Shaw's Hiker Hostel

    Well-known hostel and resupply depot for the Appalachian Trail, offering private and shared/bunkhouse rooms plus shuttle service. Onsite amenities include laundry, Wi-Fi, and a full resupply store stocked with trail food and gear. They'll also hold resupply packages for thru-hikers to pick up. A popular last stop to rest, shower, and stock up before or after the Hundred Mile Wilderness.

    Stay safe

    Monson is known for its slate mines and quarries. Many of them are out of use and flooded now. If you are going to be nearby these abandoned quarries, be aware that they can be hard to self-rescue out of. Recovery attempts can be hard or impossible due to water conditions in the quarries and challenges around draining them. Do not jump into unknown quarries, and never swim alone.
