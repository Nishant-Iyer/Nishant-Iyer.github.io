Overview

Woodbridge is a town in Prince William County and suburb of Washington, D.C., best known for the Potomac Mills shopping mall.

Understand

While many people visit Woodbridge to shop at Potomac Mills, the historic town of Occoquan is a lovely day trip and there are several great restaurants too.

Get in

By car

Woodbridge is located just off I-95, in the Washington D.C. - Richmond corridor, with the main drag along US-1.

By train

Go Woodbridge station

The Woodbridge Amtrak train station is served by the Northeast Regional train, which travels north to Washington, D.C., Baltimore, Philadelphia, New York City, and Boston, and travels south to Richmond, and either Williamsburg or Norfolk. There are 3 trains per day. The journey to Washington, D.C., takes 35 minutes and costs $21 if not purchased well in advance. The station is located near Route 1, and it is not easy to leave the station on foot. This station is also served by VRE commuter trains between Fredericksburg and DC.

To/from Potomac Mills Shopping Mall

For more information on riding the Metrorail and using a SmarTrip card, see Washington DC#Get_In.

  • Prince William Metro Direct Circular operates between Potomac Mills and the Franconia-Springfield metro station. The trip from the Franconia-Springfield metro takes 30 minutes and costs $3.60 if paid using a SmarTrip card. The bus operates every day except Sunday.
  • Dale City-Washington OmniRide operates between Potomac Mills and the White House and the Pentagon (Bus Bay L1). The bus operates only on weekdays and costs $5.75 if paid using a SmarTrip card. The first buses from Washington leave in the early afternoon, but the journey to Potomac Mills takes only 45-50 minutes, which is faster than taking the Prince William Metro Direct Circular.

Eat Ricos Tacos Moya

Disproportionately Mexican clientele and only one server who really speaks English (the daughter), and some incredible food. Highlights are the tacos themselves and the menudo, which is not available every day. They also serve the rare-to-D.C. micheladas—beer with spices, salt rim, and often tabasco sauce and clam juice (clamada). They're a lot of fun.
