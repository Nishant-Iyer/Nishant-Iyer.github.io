Overview

Often considered the "mother city" of the Cajun settlements in Louisiana, St. Martinville is the parish seat of St. Martin Parish and a picturesque Main Street community. The centerpiece of the town is the St. Martin de Tours Catholic Church, which was the center of both community and religious life for the Acadian settlers that came to south central Louisiana.

Best things about St. Martinville

Longfellow-Evangeline State Historic Site

This park features the Creole plantation house Maison Olivier, an Acadian cabin, and an Acadian farmstead with slave quarters on the Bayou Teche.

Saint Martin De Tours Church

Church erected by Acadian refugees around 1840 that is more or less the centerpiece of St. Martinville.

St. Martinville Cultural Heritage Center

Contains the African-American Museum and the Museum of the Acadian Memorial.

Evangeline Oak Park

Here you'll find the Evangeline Oak which figures as the local legendary meeting place of the star-crossed lovers from the poem Evangeline by Henry Longfellow, a rather sacrosanct novelty in this town.

Lake Martin

Some more very aesthetic swamp scenes here. If you have a boat, you can immerse yourself in this environment with Spanish moss covered cypress trees and quiet waters.

    Sleep

    The most authentic experience would probably be staying in cabins or a tent at Lake Fausse Pointe State Park or similarly in a tent at Champagne's Swamp Tours, but there is also a bed and breakfast on Lake Martinville for those who want more comforts.
