Overview

Panaca is a city of a thousand people (2010) in Southern Nevada. It is one of only two cities in Nevada that prohibits gambling, the other being Boulder City.

    Understand

    It was founded as a Mormon settlement in 1864, making it the oldest settlement in Southern Nevada. The town has not changed much since.

    History

    Coke ovens here once produced charcoal for the smelters in nearby Bullionville (now a ghost town), but the town's economy is predominantly agricultural.

    The name "Panaca" comes from the Southern Paiute word Pan-nuk-ker, which means "metal, money, wealth". William Hamblin, a Mormon missionary to the Paiutes, established the Panacker Ledge (Panaca Claim) silver mine there in 1864.

    Do Cathedral Gorge State Park

    Cathedral Gorge is a fun gorge of bentonite clay that has been eroded into tall, intricate formations. Although all the hikes are relatively short, they afford visitors the chance to explore the cathedral-like spires for which the park is famous. The most accessible areas include Cathedral Caves, Moon Caves, and Canyon Caves, although these aren't really caves, but more like narrow slot canyons. Some of them go quite deep and have dark, underground sections. Wildlife includes lots of rabbits and desert spiny lizards. The park also offers a Dutch oven cook-off in April (https://parks.nv.gov/events/dutch-oven-cook-off-at-cathedral-gorge).

    1 mile north on U.S. 93, west on Cathedral Gorge State Park Road🕒 24 hrs💲 $5 per vehicle, Camping $15/night, plus $10/night for utility hookups. Additional surcharge for vehicles with non-Nevada plates☎ +1 775-728-8101⚠ Last checked Jul 2020 — confirm before you go

    Sleep

    Stay Cathedral Gorge Campground

    22 campsites, mainly oriented towards RVs, but tent camping is also possible. Wheelchair-accessible sites are available. Restrooms with flush toilets and showers. Firewood $5 per armload (bring exact change), but check the current fire warning level. Cell reception inconsistent, wifi is available for a fee. Direct access to hiking trails.

    Go next

    • Pioche — 12 mi north of Panaca on the US-319, US-93 (Great Basin Highway), then SR-321. The town whose name means pickaxe in French was built to mine silver and has plenty of historic buildings still standing.
    • Rachel — 98 mi west of Panaca on the SR-319, US-93 (Great Basin Highway), then SR-375 (Extraterrestrial Highway). A little village that is notable for its proximity to Area 51 and the Nellis Air Force base.
    • Ely — 108 mi north of Panaca on the US-319, US-93 (Great Basin Highway), then US-50. A ghost town that has several museums, one of which focuses on its railway heritage.
