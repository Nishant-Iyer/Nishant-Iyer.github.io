Overview

Ladysmith is the largest city in Rusk County, Wisconsin. With a 2020 population of roughly 3200, it is also the county seat of Rusk County. Ladysmith is the only city on the main fork of the Flambeau River and there are several parks along the river as it bends through the city.

Understand

Ladysmith, Wisconsin, was founded in the late 19th century as a logging town along the Flambeau River and grew with the arrival of the Soo Line Railroad. Originally called Flambeau Falls, it was renamed in 1900 after the wife of a railroad official. As the timber industry declined, the city shifted to agriculture, manufacturing, and tourism. A major tornado in 2002 caused significant damage, but the community rebuilt and persevered. Today, Ladysmith is known for its scenic beauty, local businesses, and events like the Northland Mardi Gras.

Go next

  • There are many more lakes along the WIS 27 corridor, especially around Hayward. Alternatively, travelers can drive south to WIS 29 in Cadott and then either go west to Chippewa Falls or east toward Wausau for more urban-like environments on the freeway.
  • St. Croix Falls on US 8 to the west has more scenic river country in addition to being the starting terminus of the 1000+ mile long Ice Age Trail.
  • Rhinelander also has an abundance of lakes for recreations; the city is 88 miles away to the east on US 8.
  • To the northeast along the North Fork Flambeau River is Park Falls.
