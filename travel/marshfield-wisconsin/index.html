Overview

Marshfield, in North Central Wisconsin, was a thriving regional center for northwest Wisconsin in the late 19th century known as "Hub City" for all of the railroad tracks that converged there. It sported a bevy of German immigrants and a strong lumber trade. Not to be outdone by Chicago, it had its own conflagration in 1877 that burned down most of the town, then reemerged with more of a brick facade. Today, it is a little quieter but nonetheless known for the Marshfield Clinic, a big name in healthcare.

Best things about Marshfield

Wildwood Zoo

A free municipal zoo with some handsome North American species like bison, cougar, lynx, and red-crowned cranes.

World's Largest Round Barn

Self-acclaimed record holder for this particular type of farm structure. No doubt it is grandiose.

Governor William H. Upham House

The former home of the particular Wisconsin statesman, with guided tours to demonstrate furnishings and life reflective of the late 19th century.
