Overview

Stratford is a town in Connecticut. Stratford, Connecticut was founded in 1639. It is in New England and located on the Long Island Sound. It is east of the Housatonic River and it also part of Fairfield County. Stratford is 18.7 miles and has a population of 51,384 people. Stratford is only an hour in driving distance from parts New York. Stratford consists of multiple public bathing beaches, five marinas, fishing piers, and two public boat launching facilities. Stratfords' mission statement is as follows, "To enhance the quality of life within the community by providing services with professionalism and a commitment to excellence." It is quite noticeable driving through the town of Stratford that the town sticks to their mission statement.

Get around

Many of the shopping plazas and popular parts of town are located on Main Street. Walking on Main Street is the most common way of getting around. There is also a bus that runs through the town 24 hours a day, 7 days a week. You can find a bus stop on almost any street corner. Also, many people bike around the town. Be sure to check out some of the bike trails!

See Sikorsky Memorial Airport (BDR)

Stratford is the birthplace of the American helicopter industry. In 1939 Igor Sikorsky successfully flew the first helicopter at his Stratford based helicopter plant. This airport is the hub of corporate activity and provides commuter service to other cities. Sikorsky Aircraft is the leading manufacturer of helicopters. Here helicopters are built for military and commercial applications.

See Capt. David Judson House

This historic house was built by David Judson in 1723 and was listed on the National Register of Historic Places. This museum is open to the public and has original furnishings from the 18th century. It is also believed that the cellar was used as slave quarters in the 18th century.

See Sterling Homestead

Sterling Homestead was created by John William Sterling in 1886. This mansion was designed by Bruce Price who designed halls at Yale University. After the death of Cordelia Sterling, the house was donated and is now used as a community center to Stratford, Connecticut.

See Connecticut Air & Space Center

Set in buildings 6 and 53 at the former Stratford Army Engine Plant complex, part of a storied history of aircraft manufacturing since WWII, where the Vought Corsair and Sirkorsky S-60 "Skycrane" helicopter and Wright radial engines were built. Now this museum is one part display, and two parts workshop, for lovingly restored warbirds. Visitors must be 18 or older for admission. Check in at nearby 550 Main St for a briefing and clearance, before visiting the museum.
