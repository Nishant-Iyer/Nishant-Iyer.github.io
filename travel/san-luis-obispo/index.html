Overview

San Luis Obispo, also SLO or San Luis to locals, is a college town and the hub of San Luis Obispo County. It is one of California's oldest communities, with a carefully preserved and bustling 19th-century downtown centered around a meticulously restored 18th-century mission, surrounded by the natural beauty of the Central Coast.

    Understand

    Nine small but impressive volcanic peaks march down a valley of the Santa Lucia range to the Pacific Ocean. Nestled around two of these peaks is San Luis Obispo. The town is clustered at the feet of the 1,000 ft Cerro San Luis, marked with a big white "M", for Mission High School. This refers to Mission San Luis Obispo de Tolosa (Saint Louis the Bishop of Toulouse), the fifth Catholic mission founded in California, and the nucleus that the city has developed around since 1772.

    San Luis Obispo is bounded by the imposing line of Cuesta Ridge to the northeast, the Irish Hills to the southwest, dramatic Bishop Peak to the northwest and Islay Hill to the southeast. The downtown area runs from Cerro San Luis roughly toward campus around two parallel streets, Higuera and Monterey.

    California Polytechnic State University (Cal Poly) is just outside city limits, at the foot of Cuesta Ridge with its big white "P". The school has about 23,000 students, a substantial fraction of the city's population of about 50,000.

    Get in

    SLO is about halfway between Los Angeles, 200 mi to the south, and San Francisco, 230 mi to the north.

    By plane

    Go San Luis Obispo County Regional Airport (SBP)

    A one-terminal airport with scheduled flights from Dallas, Denver, Los Angeles, Portland, Phoenix, San Francisco, and Seattle on Alaska Air, American Airlines, and United. Taxis and Uber/Lyft is available, as well as rental cars, and the 1A and 1B city bus routes link the airport to downtown.

    ⚠ Last checked Jun 2021 — confirm before you go
    MapsWebsite

    The next closest commercial airport is Santa Maria Public Airport (SMX), about 35 mi south.

    From Los Angeles (LAX), the nearest major international airport, the Central Coast Shuttle has 7 trips to Santa Maria Airport. This is a limited option, an Uber or ride from a friend must be procured, but may work in your situation.

    Air Charter or air taxi is another alternative, where you can catch flights to San Diego for the weekend, or to any large or small airport. Air charters and air taxis fly point-to-point, at the departure time, departure airport, and arrival airport of the passengers' choosing. Prices start around $150 for a seat on flights that might already be going to your destination, or go from $1,000 to $10,000 or more for a custom-chartered flight where you hire the aircraft to fly you directly to your destination, any time you want to go. On-demand flights, and seats on flights already chartered to/from San Luis Obispo are offered by Pacific Air Taxi. Air taxi flights have a much shorter check-in time (typically just 15 minutes) than larger airlines, so you can just arrive and go. Travel time for an air taxi flight is usually 80 minutes from SLO to Sacramento.

    By car

    Since San Luis Obispo is home to the first ever motel (The Milestone Mo-Tel) it is definitely a car town.

    U.S. Highway 101 (El Camino Real) runs through town, from Pismo Beach and all the way to Los Angeles, as well as inland over the Cuesta Grade to Atascadero and Paso Robles, then onward through the Salinas Valley to the San Francisco Bay Area.

    San Luis Obispo is the south end of dramatic Big Sur stretch of the Pacific Coast Highway (California Highway 1). The PCH joins US-101 in town, overlapping from San Luis Obispo until splitting again further south in Pismo Beach. Travellers opting for the very worthwhile scenery of Big Sur via the PCH should be aware that driving times are much longer than by the 101.

    Get around

    Car is the easiest way to get around town and the larger region, but many locals opt to travel by bike or to walk within the city.

    By bus

    SLO Transit Services are reliable and cover most points of interest, but end at 11PM on weekdays and 8PM on the weekends. A trolley links some of the hotels on Monterey Street to the downtown area.

    Regional Transit Authority The county bus system. Mainly intended for travel to neighboring cities, with limited service inside SLO, but check if the stops and schedules work for you.

    By foot

    Walking is a great way to get around, especially if you're staying close to downtown.

    By bike

    SLO keeps improving its bike infrastructure every year. Several bike shops offer rentals and some hotels have bike available for use by guests.

    See

    Take some time to walk around downtown. While there are some new developments and larger chain restaurants/stores, it still maintains a wonderful small town feel, with pedestrian corridors between streets where you'll find locals hanging out.

    See Mission San Luis Obispo de Tolosa

    Founded in 1772 by Junipero Serra as the fifth Catholic mission in California. Still an active parish, it is in the middle of downtown on Chorro St and Monterey St, with its main courtyard interrupting Monterey St for a full city block. In addition to the church, there is a small museum; both are definitely worth visiting. The Mission is part of the Mission Plaza development, which incorporates San Luis Creek and a number of stores and restaurants. Walk from the Mission to the creek and up to one of the restaurants for a bite to eat.

    Eat

    Most restaurants, bars and shops are on Higuera St, nearer the Mission and Cerro San Luis. A few restaurants are in the charming Railroad District, about 5 blocks east of Higuera via Santa Rosa St, near the train station and youth hostel.

    Dining in town will set you back a few dollars. Consequently, a fun and satisfying way to explore downtown is to have an itinerant dinner, sampling appetizers, salads, and a main course or two at a variety of eateries.

    The Central Coast is the home of the tri-tip steak, also known as Santa Maria Barbecue. A lightly seasoned beef roast, where the fat hasn't been trimmed off, is cooked slowly over an oak fire, so the fat melts and tenderizes the meat. Look for a tri-tip sandwich at places that serve your usual burgers and grilled fare.

    Drink

    The downtown area has many bars along Higuera St south of Santa Rosa Ave, owing to the large student population. They are all an easy walk (or stumble) from one another. These start to become active around 10PM and on weekends will generally be quite busy up until 2AM. Options range from Martini lounge to Irish pub to wannabe LA nightclub.

    Wine

    There are several award-winning wineries in SLO County, many of which offer free wine tasting. Brochures to guide you through the area are available all over town, but they are actually unnecessary.

    • There are a number of wine tasting bars downtown.
    • Follow Broad St out of town and the vineyards of Edna Valley will begin to appear on the left. In addition to the large namesake establishment, over a dozen smaller, high quality wineries are open for tasting. Explore the region between Edna Valley Road and Orcutt Road to the east.
    • Drive north on US-101 into Paso Robles and turn either direction on Highway 46. There will be wineries on either side of the road.

    Go next

    • Carrizo Plain National Monument - 75 miles (120 km) east, this remote national monument is home to the largest remaining native grassland in California. During wet years it boasts spectacular wildflowers in the spring, and Soda Lake is an important wintering area for waterfowl when precipitation is sufficient to flood the seasonal lakebed. The park is also home to Painted Rock, a Native American site that has been adorned with pictographs over thousands of years. Services are extremely limited, with just a small visitor center, two primitive campgrounds, and a handful of marked trails available.
    • Los Osos - The gateway to Montana de Oro State Park, one of the state's best-kept secrets. It's filled with hiking, mountain biking, and equestrian trails both along the ocean along the bluffs and inland into the coastal peaks; a dramatic cove with caves that kids and adults love; and miles of sandy beach and tidal pools.
    • Paso Robles - Paso Robles is a historical resort town known for its hot springs, 31 miles (50 km) north. This city's major industry is wine production, with vineyards located throughout the surrounding area.

    Take the Pacific Coast Highway north to Big Sur:

    • Morro Bay - 13 miles (21 km) northwest, this coastal town is best known for the massive 581-foot (177 m) volcanic rock that dominates its harbor, but is also a great spot to see marine mammals and is home to one of California's oldest fine art and craft shows.
    • San Simeon - 42 miles (68 km) northwest, San Simeon is home to Hearst Castle, the impressive residence of the former media tycoon William Randolph Hearst, now managed as a state park with tours available daily. A massive colony of more than 20,000 elephant seals can be viewed onshore just north of town every winter.

    Take the Pacific Coast Highway south:

    • Avila Beach - Avila Beach is a small coastal community 10 miles (16 km) southwest that offers a number of quaint inns, spas and resorts. Point San Luis, accessible via a scenic docent-led hike or by trolley, is home to a lighthouse that dates back to 1890 and is the only surviving Prairie Victorian on the West Coast. Visitors looking for relaxation can soak in nearby mineral hot springs or lounge on the sandy beach that gives the town its name.
    • Pismo Beach - This tourism-focused coastal town 13 miles (21 km) south has more than 65 restaurants and 30 lodging options, and draws visitors with its beautiful coastline, annual clam festival, and the thousands of monarch butterflies that gather to spend the winter in a grove at the edge of town.
