Overview

Waverly, the county seat of Humphreys County in Central Tennessee, is like the typical sleepy Southern town with charm. It has points of interest covering a large spectrum of time, from the prehistoric era to the Civil War to contemporary culture.

Waverly Train Disaster Memorial

In February of 1978, Waverly was dealt a severe blow when a propane car derailed from the track and exploded, killing 16 people. This is a memorial to that occasion.

Humphrey's County Museum & Civil War Fort

Small regional museum that includes Civil War events that took place around Waverly.

Loretta Lynn's Ranch

A themed park surrounding the home where this country star used to live. It even includes a Coal Miner's Daughter Museum.

Benton-Houston Ferry (Danville Ferry)

For something quirky, you could ride this ferry across Kentucky Lake, and in so doing, see a partially submerged granary in the river called the Old Danville Grain Elevator. It is a rather eerie sight.

See Johnsonville State Historic Park

Site of the Civil War Battle of Johnsonville, where Maj Gen Nathan Bedford Forrest wreaked havoc on the Union fort here, hamstringing the North's efforts to execute its campaign. Additionally, it is the site of the former township of Johnsonville that was inundated when Kentucky Lake was formed. Nice interpretive trails along the lake cover these topics. The park also manages the Link Farm State Archaelogical Area that is somewhat to the southeast, where a large discovery of Mississipian burial mounds from 1100AD was uncovered and where Jesse James ironically once farmed.
