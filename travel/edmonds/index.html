Overview

Edmonds is a city on the Puget Sound of Washington State, between Seattle and Everett. A walkable main street and seaside parks frame the busy ferry terminal. The city is charming enough for European travel personality Rick Steves, who makes his home here.

    Get in

    By car

    State Route 99 (SR-99), the Pacific Highway, runs just inside the eastern edge of the city.

    Interstate 5 runs just a mile east of the city. Northbound, take Exit 177 WA-104. Southbound, take Exit 179 220th St. This is usually the best way to the city from Seattle.

    By ferry

    Washington State Ferries (WSF) operates the Edmonds / Kingston route, greatly shortening the distance needed to drive to the Kitsap Peninsula (and the Olympic Peninsula) from the Seattle metro area.

    Go Edmonds Terminal

    Connects Kingston to Edmonds. 30 minutes one way, departures about once an hour. All boats on this route accept both walk on and vehicle traffic.

    📍 199 Sunset Ave S☎ +1 206-464-6400Checked May 2026
    MapsWebsite

    Fares are one-way for vehicles, and round-trip for passengers. Fares are calculated based on sailing time and season, type and length of vehicle and the amount and ages of passengers. For example, fares during a summer weekend in 2026 are:

    • $27.00 for a standard 14-22 ft car and its driver
    • $11.35 for additional adult passengers and walk-ons from Edmonds (free from Kingston)
    • $1.00 for each ride-on bicycle from Edmonds, in addition to the passenger fare (free from Kingston)

    Ferry lines for vehicles can get long on summer weekends, so arrive early or prepare to wait a few hours.

    Scuba diving

    Although the colder waters of Puget Sound are not for everyone, the Edmonds Underwater Park is one of the most popular shore dives in the state for scuba divers.

    Do Edmonds Underwater Park

    The park is relatively shallow with a maximum depth of about 45 feet. There is a network of anchored ropes that lead to a variety of submerged features. Shipwrecks include the large tugboat Triumph and the Lopez Pontoon . The site is known for large ling cod, cabezon, octopus, surf perch and rockfish, and a lot of invertebrates. Grey whales have also been seen by divers.
