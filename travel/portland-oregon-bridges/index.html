See Broadway Bridge

📍 Broadway between the Pearl District and Rose QuarterChecked Sep 2026

See Steel Bridge

Double-deck vertical lift bridge; the lower deck is raised frequently and the upper deck from time to time. Current version dates from 1912; previous version from 1888. Lower deck carries Union Pacific rail and Amtrak, upper deck carries cars (OR-99W), pedestrians, bikes, four MAX light rail lines and seven Tri-Met bus routes.

Glisan, Everett and Naito Pkwy to the West (Old Town/Chinatown), Interstate Ave, Multnomah Street and Oregon Street to the East (Rose Quarter)Checked Sep 2026
Maps

See Burnside Bridge

Bascule bridge built in 1926, replacing a bridge built in 1892. Listed on the NRHP. Accessible by car, bike, foot and three TriMet bus lines. The Portland Oregon White Stag sign is best seen from this bridge. The Portland Saturday Market is held under the west side of the bridge, and there is a skate park under the east side of the bridge. This bridge will be rebuilt in the next few years.

Burnside Street between Old Town Chinatown and BuckmanChecked Sep 2026
Maps

See Morrison Bridge

Double-leaf bascule bridge opened in 1985, replacing bridges built in 1887 and 1905. Is the main way to get from Interstate 5 into Downtown Portland. Accessible by car, bike, foot or the Route 15 bus.

Between Washington/Alder Streets in Downtown Portland and Morrison/Belmont Streets in the Central EastsideChecked Sep 2026

See Hawthorne Bridge

Vertical lift truss bridge built in 1910, replacing two earlier bridges known as the Madison Street Bridge. Is the busiest bike bridge in Portland, and also accessible by cars, foot, or three MAX bus lines.

Between Main/Madison Streets in Downtown and Madison St/Hawthorne Blvd in the Central EastsideChecked Sep 2026

See Marquam Bridge

Double-decker truss/cantilever bridge opened in 1966. Open only to cars on Interstate 5. Often derided for its lack of aesthetics.

Interstate 5 between the Southwest and Southeast WaterfrontsChecked Sep 2026

See Tilkum Crossing

Cable-stayed bridge opened in 2015. NOT open to autos, but open to bikes, feet, MAX Orange Line, Portland Streetcar, and four TriMet bus lines

Between Porter & Moody on the Southwest Waterfront and Water Street and Caruthers Street on Southeast WaterfrontsChecked Sep 2026

See Ross Island Bridge
