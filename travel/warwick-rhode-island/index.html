Overview

Warwick is a city in Rhode Island just south of Providence. It is the third largest city in the state, with 83,000 people (2020). It was founded in 1642 by Samuel Gorton, and played an important role in the Revolutionary War. It is popular among tourists for its colonial history and its location near beaches and saltwater bays.

    Understand

    Historically the second-largest city in the state of Rhode Island, Warwick has fallen to third place due to the growth of neighboring Cranston. While Warwick remains a "big city" by Rhode Island standards, don't be fooled: it's almost entirely suburban in character.

    The city is known for both its colonial history and its beaches; at Conimicut Point, the Providence River widens to form New England's largest estuary, Narragansett Bay, which gives the city the northernmost saltwater beaches in the Ocean State and brings many summer visitors from points north who don't want to brave the South County beach traffic. Warwick is largely defined by the water, with the city's entire eastern edge bordering the Providence River, Narragansett Bay and Greenwich Bay, and many of its attractions are on the 39-mile shoreline.

    When many Rhode Islanders hear "Warwick," they think of the residential neighborhoods full of mid-20th-century Cape Cod houses, the traffic on Post Road near the airport, and the big-box stores that line busy Route 2. This is the Warwick right off Interstate 95. But even in this sprawling city, you don't have to look very far to find a different and considerably more charming Warwick. The city is home to two popular state parks as well as a hidden-gem city park with a tranquil beach, and that's just scratching the surface. Look a little deeper and you'll find two historic lighthouses, iconic clam shacks, stunning waterfront neighborhoods, and a serene nature preserve that may be all yours.

    Its central location within Rhode Island makes Warwick a perfect home base from which to see all of this minuscule state. From the center of town, even the farthest reaches of Little Rhody can be accessed by car in under an hour. Warwick is home to Rhode Island's main airport, T.F. Green Airport, which serves the Providence and Boston areas.

    By plane

    Go Rhode Island T.F. Green International Airport (PVD)

    With service from about a dozen airlines, T.F. Green offers domestic connections to numerous destinations east of the Mississippi. Despite the name, there were no international flights in 2022. Amtrak doesn't stop here, but the airport is connected to Boston via the MBTA Providence Line, which runs about 10 trains daily, one every two hours give or take. A skywalk connects the terminal with the train station, and walking between the two takes about 10 min. A one-way trip costs about $13 (2022). Car rental options are available; Budget, Enterprise, Alamo, and the like. However, rentals here tend to be more expensive than at Boston Logan. You're adjacent to the I-95 corridor, where gas stations and other traveller necessities abound.

    Villages

    There are many interesting colonial villages located throughout the city of Warwick.

    • Apponaug Village Located off Route 1. Apponaug Village is a historic center settled in 1696. It was the center of trade and politics after the Revolutionary War, and was an important shipping port to Rhode Island. Today it is commonly referred to Warwick’s downtown area, and the city is actively working on improving its walkability and amenities. Included in the village are Warwick City Hall, multiple churches, and the Warwick museum.
    • Conimicut Village Anchored along West Shore Road, Conimicut Village’s history can be traced back to the founding of Warwick in 1643. It was once a fishing village, but today features Conimicut Point Park and Conimicut lighthouse. While the lighthouse is not open to the public, it is still in use and is a historical structure. Conimincut Point Park is open to the public and is a great place to visit or take a drive through on a nice summer day.
    • Pawtuxet Village Located off Broad Street at the northernmost point of the city, and shared with Cranston -- a charming bridge over the Pawtuxet River connects the two cities -- Pawtuxet Village is the oldest village in New England, and one of the first constructed in Warwick. It is where rabble-rousers once burned the British ship, The Gaspee, at the beginning of the American Revolutionary War. It was also one of the stops on the Underground Railroad for runaway slaves before and during the Civil War. Walking tours of the village are available to the public. The village is also home to many small shops and restaurants. Many people visit this village during the summer when they are able to take in all that the village has to offer.

Many programs are held at the library featuring musicians, artists, and story tellers. The Warwick Public Library also offers free museum passes to many attractions around Rhode Island and beyond.     The Aldrich mansion was built in 1896 for Senator Nelson W. Aldrich. It spreads over 75 acres and overlooks Narragansett Bay. The French chateau has 70 rooms and features a long marble staircase, antique furniture, elaborate paintings and wood carvings. It includes a Carriage House, Caretaker’s Cottage and Boathouse.

    Do Morris Farm and Greenhouses

    Founded in 1915, this 43-acre farm is an oasis in bustling suburbia. Morris Farm offers seasonal activities ranging from holiday decoration making in December to hayrides and a corn maze in October and also hosts school tours, birthday parties. Lines form at the farm stand which sells fresh, local produce. Locals often leave it up to Mrs. Morris to make their holiday pies and side dishes.

    Stay safe

    Warwick is a very safe community and little violence or crime is committed. Feeling safe in this city is something that you don’t have to worry about.

    If you're looking for additional activities just outside of Warwick some additional places of interest include AMF Lanes (1450 Elmwood Ave, Cranston) and Roger Williams Park (also accessible from Elmwood Ave.) The Providence Place Mall is 20 minutes away and is a great destination for shopping as the mall is larger than the one in Warwick.
