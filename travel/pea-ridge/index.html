Overview

Pea Ridge, located in the picturesque Arkansas Ozarks region, is a town steeped in rich history and natural beauty. Renowned as the site of the pivotal Battle of Pea Ridge during the American Civil War, the area now hosts one of the best-preserved battlefields in the United States, the Pea Ridge National Military Park. This quaint town offers visitors a unique blend of historical exploration and scenic charm.

Understand

The Battle of Pea Ridge, fought from March 7-8, 1862, was a defining moment in the American Civil War. It was the largest battle west of the Mississippi River, involving 16,000 Confederate soldiers and 10,500 Union troops. The fierce confrontation resulted in approximately 3,384 casualties and a significant victory for Union forces, ensuring federal control over Missouri.

The town's layout commemorates the soldiers who fought in the battle:

  • East-west streets are named after Confederate soldiers.
  • North-south streets honor Union soldiers.

This unique tribute reflects the town's deep connection to its history, making it a living memorial to the Civil War era.

Get in

Pea Ridge is easily accessible by road, making it a convenient destination for history buffs and travelers exploring the Arkansas Ozarks.

  • Arkansas Highway 94: The main highway passes through the town from the south to the northwest.
  • Highway 72: This road connects Pea Ridge to U.S. Route 62, the primary route to the Pea Ridge National Military Park.
  • Nearby Cities: The town is a short drive from larger cities like Bentonville and Rogers, providing easy access to additional amenities.

Get around

Pea Ridge is a small town that is best explored by car, especially when visiting the Pea Ridge National Military Park, located about five miles east of the town center. The park features well-maintained roads, walking trails, and cycling paths, ensuring an immersive experience for visitors.

See

1. Pea Ridge National Military Park

  • Location: 15930 E Highway 62
  • Contact: ☏ +1 479-451-8122
  • Hours: Open daily from 6 AM to sunset. Visitor Center: 8:30 AM – 4:30 PM.
  • Details: Established in 1956, the Pea Ridge National Military Park spans over 4,300 acres (approximately 17 km²), preserving the core sites of the historic 1862 battle. Key highlights include:
  • Elkhorn Tavern: A restored structure that served as the focal point during the battle.
  • Telegraph Road: Sections of the pre-war road used during the Civil War.
  • Museum and Visitor Center: Features exhibits, artifacts, and detailed accounts of the battle.
  • Battlefield Tour: A self-guided driving loop with interpretive stops at significant locations.

This park offers an unparalleled opportunity to step back in time and understand the strategic importance of the battle.

Do

  • Hiking and Biking Explore the military park’s trails, which range from easy walks to more challenging hikes. Cyclists can enjoy the paved roads within the park.
  • Historical Reenactments Check the park’s schedule for events, including Civil War reenactments and educational programs that bring history to life.
  • Photography Capture stunning landscapes of the preserved battlefield, especially during sunrise or sunset. The Elkhorn Tavern and rolling hills provide excellent backdrops.

Buy

While Pea Ridge is a small town, the visitor center gift shop at the National Military Park offers:

  • Civil War-themed souvenirs.
  • Books detailing the history of the battle.
  • Locally made crafts and keepsakes.

For more extensive shopping, nearby cities like Bentonville and Rogers have malls, boutiques, and antique stores.

Sleep

For overnight stays, Pea Ridge has limited options, but nearby Bentonville and Rogers offer accommodations ranging from budget motels to luxury hotels.

  • Camping: Enthusiasts can explore camping options at parks like Hobbs State Park, a short drive away.
  • Bed & Breakfasts: Quaint lodgings in the Ozarks region provide a more personalized experience.

Go next

After exploring Pea Ridge, consider visiting:

  • Hobbs State Park-Conservation Area: Perfect for hiking, fishing, and kayaking.
  • Eureka Springs: A charming town known for its Victorian architecture and natural springs.
