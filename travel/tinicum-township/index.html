Understand

Tinicum Township is located in the southeastern part of Delaware County southwest of the city of Philadelphia. The township is made up of the communities of Essington and Lester. The Philadelphia International Airport is located in the eastern part of the township on the border with Philadelphia. Tinicum Township is home to hotels and off-site airport parking facilities.

Get in

By car

Interstate 95 passes through Tinicum Township and leads north to Philadelphia and south to Chester and Wilmington. Exit 9 of Interstate 95 with Pennsylvania Route 420 provides access to the township. Pennsylvania Route 291 runs east-west through Tinicum Township along Industrial Highway and Governor Printz Boulevard, leading north to Philadelphia and south to Chester. Pennsylvania Route 420 heads north-south through the township on Wanamaker Avenue and leads north to Prospect Park and Springfield.

By plane

Philadelphia International Airport (PHL) is located on the eastern border of Tincum Township and offers a large selection of flights.

By bus

Place SEPTA Route 37

SEPTA provides bus service to Tinicum Township along Route 37, which operates through the township along Industrial Highway and Governor Printz Boulevard and heads north to the Philadelphia International Airport and Broad Street and Snyder Avenue in South Philadelphia and south to the Chester Transportation Center in Chester. The Route 37 bus offers connections to the Broad Street Line subway at Snyder Avenue, SEPTA Regional Rail's Airport Line at the airport, and SEPTA Regional Rail's Wilmington/Newark Line at the Chester Transportation Center, all three of which provide service to Center City Philadelphia.

Get around

See

See John Heinz National Wildlife Refuge at Tinicum

Located on the border of Tinicum Township and Philadelphia, the John Heinz National Wildlife Refuge at Tinicum is the first urban National Wildlife Refuge in the United States and preserves the Tinicum Marsh along the Darby Creek, which is the largest freshwater tidal marsh remaining in Pennsylvania. The refuge is a stop for birds along the Atlantic Flyway and is home for a variety of wildlife including deer, fox, raccoons, butterflies, fish, ducks, geese, osprey, and eagles. The John Heinz National Wildlife Refuge at Tinicum features a 10-mile network of trails that allow for viewing of wildlife. You can also fish in the Darby Creek of travel through the marsh in a canoe. Also located at the refuge is the Cusano Environmental Education Center.
