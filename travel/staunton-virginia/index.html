Overview

Staunton is a city in the Shenandoah Valley in the US state of Virginia. Although small, it is very distinct from neighboring cities such as Harrisonburg, Roanoke, and Waynesboro, in part due to the city government's emphasis on tourism and the very distinct downtown district.

Get around

The town is small enough that if you stay downtown, you can walk anywhere you would like to go. There is also a trolley/bus service provided by the city for $0.25 per ride.

See Camera Heritage Museum

Billing itself as "The largest camera museum open to the public in the USA", the museum collection contains over 7000 cameras, many of them antiques. Cameras are the focus here, but some of the photos on display also convey a sense of Staunton's history.

See Landes Park

This small park (approx. 1 acre) nestled between roads, train tracks, and the creek is easy to miss. It's noteworthy, however, for the photogenic stone arches that form part of the railway bridge over the southern half of the park.

Do Virginia Scenic Railway

Take a three-hour scenic tour on a restored historic train. Departures from Staunton head west in the morning, east in the afternoon. You can also catch themed train rides: murder mystery, sip&savor, etc. Prices vary. Reserve your tickets in advance - there are no walkup sales.

📍 415 Bridge St
Google MapsWebsite

Do Sears Hill Bridge

This bridge over the train tracks by the Amtrak Station was built to link the Sears Hill neighborhood with downtown. Today it provides a great view of downtown's architecture and geography. Enjoy the view right from the bridge, or head just a short distance up the hill for an overlook complete with information boards explaining which buildings are which.

Google Maps

Do Take a Walking Tour

Ask at the Staunton Visitor Center (35 S New St). Most days there are free general tours or architecture tours, typically leaving mid-morning. Sometimes there are also special themed tours (fees vary). If you miss the guided tours, the visitor center also has maps of self-guided tours through several of the historic downtown neighborhoods, with detailed information on the notable homes and architecture.

📍 35 S New St

Drink

Drink Queen City Brewing

A unique storefront which carries on a fine Southern tradition: making your own alcohol. Patrons can use the facility to create their own beer or wine, which are aged at the facility until ready to be picked up. For those not so DIY-inclined, Queen City Brewery also offers their own specialty beers and lagers, fruit wines, and non-alcoholic soda pops. Friday nights are "Brewgrass Fridays", and local bluegrass bands play while the storefront offers free samples. No cover.

📍 834 Springhill Rd☎ +1 540-213-8014
Google MapsWebsite

Drink Ciders from Mars

Grab a glass, a flight, or a take-home bottle of craft-brewed alcoholic cider. Usually 6+ flavors on tap, specializing in dry and off-dry ciders fermented from local VA apples. In summertime, the cider slushie is a great treat! (Note: Address correct as of July 2026, but they're hoping to relocate their taproom elsewhere in Staunton by the end of the year.)

Go next

  • "Monticello", home of Thomas Jefferson, is in Charlottesville a 45-minute drive away.
  • "Grand Caverns" is a large show cave with spectacular shield formations and reflecting pools. 20-25 minutes to the northeast of downtown.
  • The Blue Ridge Parkway and Shenandoah Valley National Park's Skyline Drive are nearby. The park costs $30 per car in 2026. The Blue Ridge Parkway is free. Great scenic overlooks and stopping points for small historical recreations or hikes.
