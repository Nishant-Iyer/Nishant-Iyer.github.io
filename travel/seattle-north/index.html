Overview

North Seattle is roughly the area between NE 85th St and the border with Shoreline at NE 145th St. This area was outside of city limits until 1954, so it developed in a distinctly suburban manner. The area is characterized by homes with larger lot sizes and strip malls along the highways radiating out from the city, especially I-5 at Northgate, Aurora Ave, and Lake City Way. This article also includes a few neighborhoods south of 85th St that face Lake Washington, as these areas are also more suburban and residential than the nearby University District.

    Understand

    The neighborhoods in North Seattle include, from west to east:

    • Crown Hill, just north of Ballard
    • Broadview, a residential neighborhood north of Carkeek Park
    • Greenwood, at Greenwood Ave and 85th St
    • Bitter Lake, just west of Aurora Ave
    • Licton Springs, along Aurora Ave north of Green Lake
    • Haller Lake, a residential neighborhood between Aurora Ave and I-5
    • Maple Leaf, along 5th Ave NE near 90th St
    • Northgate, at Northgate Way and I-5. It was the site of one of the first shopping malls in the US, ajd is still a major retail and transit hub.
    • Pinehurst
    • Victory Heights
    • Lake City, along Lake City Way, host of some creepy dive bars, thrift/pawn stores, and used car dealerships, as well as new urban development.
    • Wedgwood

    Some people define "North Seattle" more broadly, to cover everything north of the Ship Canal that bisects the city, which would also include Ballard, Fremont and the University District.

    Get in

    By car

    From Interstate 5, you can get off at Lake City Way NE, N 85th St, N Northgate Way and NE 125th St.

    Aurora Ave N (State Route 99) passes through North Seattle. This is usually the fastest way from most parts west of I-5.

    Lake City Way (State Route 522) runs diagonally from I-5 at Green Lake towards the north tip of Lake Washington and on to Lake Forest Park, Kenmore and Bothell.

    Other major north-south arterials are 15th Ave NW and Greenwood Ave N from Ballard, and 15th Ave NE and 25th Ave NE from University District.

    Get around

    By car

    Like much of Seattle, going north-south is easy, going east-west is tricky. The east-west routes that are useful on both sides of I-5 are NE Northgate Way and NE 145th Street.

    The Northeast and North road sectors are split by Interstate 5, while the Northwest and North road sectors are split by Greenwood Ave N.

    Areas south of Northgate and near the rim of water bodies are hilly, so take some caution while driving, especially when it is raining.

    By bicycle

    The Burke-Gilman Trail runs along the Ship Canal, to the University of Washington, University Village, Magnuson Park, and then along the western rim of Lake Washington. Despite the hilly surroundings, the track is flat as it used to be a railway line.

    The Interurban Trail is the former commuter rail line to Everett. It enters Seattle at 145th St along Linden Ave, one block west of Aurora Ave N, and continues as a separate trail until 109th St. From there, Fremont Ave N continues south as a signed bicycle route to the Fremont neighborhood.

    Sleep

    Budget travelers can consider staying in North Seattle, as what can be found here is generally inexpensive. However, avoid Aurora Avenue's motels especially the off-brands that quote hourly rates. Staying at the slightly pricier national chain inns, while not fully keeping you away from the sketchy areas, are still safer and saner.

    Stay safe

    Aurora Ave N, especially between 85th St and 125th St, is something of a red-light district. Walking around in the evenings is looking for trouble. The surrounding residential areas become quiet and calm again a few blocks in either direction.

    Lake City frequently cycles between up-and-coming gentrification and sketchiness. Avoid walking along Lake City Way NE at night as robbery and muggings are not uncommon (albeit isolated).

    A few areas are not well-lit by street lights, and sidewalks are much less prevalent than in other parts of the city.
