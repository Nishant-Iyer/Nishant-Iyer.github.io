    How it works

    • The problem: choose k cities as centers so that the demand-weighted distance from every city to its nearest center is as small as possible (the "p-median" problem).
    • The method: start by adding centers one at a time, each in the spot that helps most. Then keep trying to move any center to any other city, applying the best move, until no move helps. With capacity limits, the biggest cities pick first and each takes the nearest center with room.
    • Distances: by road on the same landmass (straight line times a road detour factor for each country), otherwise by road to the coast, by sea along real shipping lanes through Suez and Panama, and by road again.
    • Try: switch between road and sea and straight-line distance to see how much the oceans matter, set a running cost and let it pick how many centers to open, or cap each center at 25% of demand and watch Asia split.

    Data: the 1,000 largest cities in the dataset, across 129 countries. GDP per person, logistics scores and price levels come from the World Bank; a handful of countries it doesn't cover use the median of their income group. Read the case study or the code.
